Building the binomial

See why a probability term must be multiplied by the number of arrangements.

How this explorer works

Several arrangements can give the same count of successes. With two trials, success followed by failure and failure followed by success both give one success. The binomial formula multiplies the probability of one arrangement by the number of possible arrangements. Change p and compare the three plots.

One success/failure sequence is one outcome. A count can represent many sequences. Multiply the number of sequences by the probability of each sequence.

P(X=k)=(nk)pk(1−p)n−k
What the symbols mean

k is the count of successes in n trials. The bracketed coefficient counts arrangements. p is the success probability on each trial.

Model, assumptions and interpretation

The coefficient counts the arrangements with k successes. The factor pᵏ(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ gives the probability of each arrangement. Their product is P(X = k). The probabilities in the final plot add to 1. The first two plots show the separate factors used in that multiplication.
