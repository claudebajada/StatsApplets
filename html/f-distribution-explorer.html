F distribution

See how the degrees of freedom affect the F distribution and its critical value.

How this explorer works

F statistics compare two estimates of variability. In ANOVA, the F statistic compares variation between group means with variation within groups. Change either set of degrees of freedom to see how the distribution changes. The dashed line marks the upper 5% critical value.

F is a non-negative ratio. Its numerator and denominator degrees of freedom both affect its reference distribution. The 5% boundary is calculated from the full tail.

F=Udf1Vdf2
What the symbols mean

U and V are independent chi-squared variables, with df₁ and df₂ degrees of freedom. F is the ratio after dividing each by its own degrees of freedom.

Model, assumptions and interpretation

F is non-negative and has no upper limit. For an upper-tail variance-ratio test or ANOVA, values above the critical value lead to rejection of the null. When the numerator has fewer than two degrees of freedom, the density grows without limit near zero. The curve omits that endpoint. On a linear axis the shaded area ends at the plot edge, while the critical value uses the full distribution. On a logarithmic axis screen area is not proportional to probability; the fill is removed. The probability and critical value remain unchanged.
