Independent t test

Compare two independent group means under an equal-variance model.

How this explorer works

An independent-samples t test compares the population means of two separate groups. This version assumes equal population variances and uses the same sample standard deviation for both groups. Set a difference between the sample means, then change each group's sample size to see how precision changes.

SD describes differences between individual observations. SE describes variation of the estimated mean or mean difference between repeated studies. These controls set summary statistics; changing n does not recruit or resample participants.

What the symbols mean

x̄₁ and x̄₂ are sample means. sₚ is the pooled sample standard deviation. n₁ and n₂ are the sample sizes. The denominator estimates the standard error of the mean difference.

Model, assumptions and interpretation

This is the pooled equal-variance test for different participants in the two groups. Repeated measurements on the same participants require a paired approach. The applet gives both groups the same sample SD for illustration; the pooled test in general does not require identical sample SDs. Welch's test, which allows unequal population variances, is outside this applet's model. Here the pooled SD is s. The test has n₁ + n₂ − 2 degrees of freedom. Exact p-values assume independent observations from normal populations with equal variances. Plot 1 shows the observed mean difference and its estimated standard error. Plot 2 shows the distribution of t when the population means are equal. Choose the alternative before looking at the data.
