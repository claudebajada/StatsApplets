One-sample t test

A one-sample t test compares a sample mean with a claimed population mean. It uses the sample standard deviation to estimate the standard error. Set a nonzero mean difference, then increase the sample size to see how the estimated uncertainty and t score change.

t=x¯−μ0sn
What the symbols mean

x̄ is the sample mean and μ₀ is the null population mean. s is the sample standard deviation and n is the sample size. The denominator estimates the standard error, or SE.

The selected p-value above uses your chosen alternative hypothesis. The other p-values use the alternatives shown here. Divide the observed difference by the sample standard deviation to get the effect size. Its sign follows the order of subtraction.

Model, assumptions and interpretation

The sliders set sample statistics. Exact t-test p-values assume independent observations from a normal population. The test uses n − 1 degrees of freedom. With small samples, skewness and outliers can make its p-values unreliable. Plot 1 shows the sample mean and its estimated standard error. Plot 2 shows the distribution of t when the null is true.
